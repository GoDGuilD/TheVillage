The Village — Executive Overview

Author: Jose-Jorge HERNANDEZ · Parlee Conseiller, Inc. · 2026-10-15

What it is

The Village is a video game: a top-down adventure in the spirit of the much-loved 1991 classic A Link to the Past. The player guides Joe, a veteran returning to his home village, through fields, forests and dungeons to break a curse cast by the village's ruler. It is the first chapter of a trilogy; the second chapter, Chapel of Dreams, is already playable in beta.

The two worlds

Light World
The village and its valley, as they are. Three dungeons hold the family seals.
Dark World
The same land as the villain dreams it. Four regions — Darkness, Fire, Time, Jungle — each with a dungeon.

Key benefits

  • Proven recipe: the same methods already produced the trilogy's second game.
  • Consistent look: one palette and one generator style keep every screen coherent.
  • Low risk of regressions: automatic checks run after every change (544 today).
  • Shared universe: characters and story carry across all three games.

Where it stands

October 2026: the foundations are rebuilt and the first two playable phases are done — Joe walks, fights with sword, spin attack and shield, lifts and throws pots, and faces three kinds of enemies that drop hearts and gems. Phase 3 added the world itself: the village of El Pueblo with four houses you can enter, and four surrounding areas that scroll into each other just like the classic. Phase 4 made it feel like a real game: a title screen, the classic on-screen display, villagers to talk to and signs to read, pause and item menus, a Game Over screen and saving. Phase 5 added the first tools of the adventure - a lantern, a boomerang, a bow and bombs - with treasure chests, locked blocks, hidden heart pieces and a village shop. Phase 6 delivered the first dungeon, the Moss Shrine: eight rooms of locked doors, switches, pits and puzzles, a map and compass, a boss and the first of the three family seals. Phase 7 completed the first act: two more dungeons with new tools (dash boots and a fire rod) and bosses, the three seals opening the villain's keep, and a final confrontation that throws the hero into the Dark World. Phase 8 built that Dark World - the whole valley as the villain dreams it - and the Moon Mirror that lets the hero step back into the waking world at the same spot. Phase 9 opened the first of its four regions, Umbra: a land of darkness with rooms lit only by the hero's lantern, a grappling hook to cross chasms, a boss that must be pulled out of the air, the first of four crystals, and a shop in the dream village. Phase 10 opened Ignis, a land of lava: an ice rod that freezes enemies and turns lava into a path for a few seconds, and a fire serpent that can only be fought while frozen - the second crystal. Phase 11 opened Tempus, a land of broken time: blocks that rise and fall with a ticking clock, a hammer that pounds pegs and flips clockwork beetles, and an armoured guardian that can stop the hero in time - the third crystal. Phase 12 opened Silva, the wild jungle: flippers to swim its rivers, snapping plants, and a giant thorn-bound flower that only fire can open - the fourth and last crystal. Next: Caver's tower, the final dungeon. The plan has 16 phases ending in a Windows release.
